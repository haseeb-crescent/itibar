## What itibar is

Every hadith comes with an isnad — the chain of named people who passed it down to the book that records it. Overlay every chain of one report across all books and you get a graph from the Prophet to the books: the isnad bundle. itibar builds that graph for every one of 20,745 reports in a 715k-narration corpus (hadith-kg, CC-BY) and computes on it what the hadith sciences have always computed by hand: how many independent lines of transmission a report has (κ, a minimum vertex cut, at four narrator-grade thresholds), who every line passes through (bottlenecks — cut vertices), where it first spreads to many students (common links), the classical number class (gharīb / ʿazīz / mashhūr / mutawātir), and the grade the critics' narrator ratings imply, with and without corroboration — with a receipt for every number. The outputs are a dataset, a paper, and this site. The standing rule: the machinery computes and reports; it never issues a verdict of its own, and the site never states one.

## Why this PR exists

PR 7 (`src/itibar/site/`, `site/assets/`) built a correct and complete static site: a page for every report and every narrator on a route, Arabic search, figures, downloads, deployed to GitHub Pages within its size budget. The data layer is good and stays. The reading experience does not work. Reviewing it as a first-time reader:

1. No answer at the top. Report 319 opens with a graph and a κ table. Nowhere does it say, in words, how this hadith reached the books.
2. Jargon everywhere. κ, "κ lower", "Above matrūk", "Ṣadūq and above", "Mashhūr", "Cut vertices: None recorded", "the earliest qualifying branches", "v1 · Best single chain", "Lowest graded ordinal 10", `sahih_li_dhatihi`, "Derived codes use the hadith-kg scale". A reader without the vocabulary stops on the first line, and nothing on the page teaches the vocabulary.
3. A visible contradiction. The home page says κ = 1 for "Actions are only by intentions" (the scoped gold check); its report page shows κ 4, with no sentence saying the extra lines are the documented deviating chains.
4. The drawing tells no story. About 220 unlabelled dots in rows. The famous shape of this hadith — ʿUmar → ʿAlqama → al-Taymī → Yaḥyā b. Saʿīd, one man per generation, then a fan of students — is invisible. The threshold toggle greys dots without saying what changed. On a phone it is a blur.
5. No hierarchy. One long scroll: about 80 book accordions before the chains, 356 chains, the classical cases at the very bottom.
6. Arabic only for every name, book and text (except the gold titles). Search works only in Arabic and returns "Report 200" with no context.
7. Narrator pages are a field table and three columns of Arabic texts: no sense of who the man was or what role he plays.

## The task

Redesign the site's reading experience so that someone who knows no hadith terminology can, within a minute of landing on a report, say how it reached the books and why its classical grade is what it is — while a specialist can still reach every number and receipt — and add the English layer that makes the site usable in English.

### Who it is for, in order

1. An English-speaking reader — a curious Muslim, a student — who knows a hadith by its English wording or a fragment of it.
2. An Arabic-literate student of hadith who wants the isnad tree, the narrators and their ratings, faster and clearer than existing tools.
3. An academic in hadith studies who wants the bundle, common links and dates, the data and the method.
4. Anyone checking the work, who wants the receipt behind each number.

The core journey for all four: find a report → understand its transmission in a minute → go as deep into the evidence as they want.

## Definition of done

1. The report page, in this order:
   * Header: an English title (the translated opening), the Arabic opening, and where it is recorded — the major collections as chips with their numbers, then "+N more books".
   * At a glance: three to five plain sentences generated by templates from the record (deterministic, no model): how many companions narrate it and who; how many independent lines reach the books with everyone counted and with only narrators rated trustworthy; the bottleneck, if every line passes through one person; where it spreads (the common link, his death year, how many students carry it on); the classical grade of its best chain, attributed, beside the grade the dataset records, attributed. Four stat tiles with plain labels. Each sentence links to the specialist section that computes it.
   * The transmission tree, story view by default. Generations as rows, the Prophet at the top and the books at the bottom. Where a row holds only a few narrators, name them; collapse wide rows into labelled clusters ("26 students of Yaḥyā b. Saʿīd") that open on tap. Mark and label the bottleneck and the spreading point. A "Show the independent lines" control draws the maximum set of lines that share no narrator, each in its own colour — this is what κ means, made visible (`flow.py` already returns a maximum set of vertex-disjoint routes; store it per threshold). The reliability filter is in words — Everyone · Excluding abandoned narrators · Truthful and above · Trustworthy only — and a sentence says what changed ("With trustworthy narrators only, 2 independent lines remain; the line through X drops out because Y is rated ṣadūq"). Tapping a node opens a side panel: English and Arabic name, dates, residence, rating in words with the Arabic label, role in this report, link to the narrator page. The full graph stays as a specialist view. The story view must work on a phone; the full graph may say it is best on a larger screen. A text version of the tree (an outline list) serves screen readers.
   * Why this grade: the best chain drawn left to right as named narrators with rating badges, and the rule in one sentence ("All six narrators are rated trustworthy or better and the chain is unbroken, so the classical rules make this chain ṣaḥīḥ"). Where corroboration raised it, show the weak point and the second chain that covers it, drawn the same way. Show the two classical readings (Ibn Hajar's and Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ's) only where they differ. The dataset's recorded grade sits beside it, labelled as the dataset's; when the critics' check PR (running in parallel) lands, it will say that this grade is produced from narrator ratings by the dataset's editors — word the label so that sentence fits.
   * Where it is recorded: books grouped — the Ṣaḥīḥayn, the other Six Books, the Musnads and Sunan, later collections — with counts; groups beyond the first collapsed.
   * All chains: one readable row per chain (narrators in order, transmission words glossed, ratings as small badges), filterable by book and by companion.
   * Classical notes: gold cases, and the critics' own remarks once the critics' check lands (`out/verdicts.parquet`): an English summary and the Arabic quote.
   * For specialists: κ at each threshold with κ_lower, cut vertices, common links, number class with its definition, terminus, every receipt, and the record as downloadable JSON. Wherever a number is scoped (the gold checks), say so in words next to the unscoped one.
2. Words. A glossary written by hand in plain English, with each term's Arabic and where it comes from: isnad, matn, companion, Successor, generation (ṭabaqa), independent lines, bottleneck, common link, the number classes, the grade classes (ṣaḥīḥ li-dhātihi / li-ghayrihi, ḥasan, ḍaʿīf …), corroboration (mutābaʿa, shāhid), tadlīs and its tiers, mursal, the unnamed companion, ikhtilāṭ, and the twelve Taqrīb levels. It has its own page, and every term in the interface gets a hover/tap definition drawn from it. The interface never shows an internal identifier (`sahih_li_dhatihi`, `v1`, "ordinal", "hadith-kg scale", code numbers). κ appears only in the specialist section, always beside "independent lines". Grades always appear as the classical term with its attribution ("by the classical rules, from the narrators' ratings" / "recorded in the dataset") and a gloss — never as a bare English verdict on the hadith ("This hadith is weak").
3. The narrator page: the English name (transliteration and the common short name, e.g. al-Zuhrī) beside the Arabic full name; one line of identity (generation in words, death year AH and CE, residence and place of death where recorded); the rating on a twelve-step scale, with the Arabic label and its meaning in plain English; tadlīs tier and conditional rulings in words; his role in transmission — common link in N reports, bottleneck in M, on a route in K — with English titles; his main teachers and students, from the bundle edges, linked; and what the critics said about him (the `aqwal` table: count, and the statements in Arabic with each critic's name).
4. Search and browse. One search box for English and Arabic: English over the translations, the transliterated names and the book titles; Arabic as now, diacritic-insensitive. Result cards carry the English title, the Arabic opening, the major collections, the number of independent lines and the classical grade. Filters: topic, collection, grade, number class. Browse by topic (the source's topic tree), by collection, by notable narrator (the common-link census), and famous reports (the gold set and the most widely narrated). The home page: one sentence on what this is, the search box, the worked example as a 60-second walkthrough of one report, and the browse entry points.
5. The English layer (data).
   * The glossary, interface copy and Taqrīb level meanings: written by hand.
   * Titles for all 425 books in English (standard English titles where they exist).
   * An English transliteration for every narrator with a page, in the IJMES / EI3 convention (ʿ, ʾ, macrons), plus the common short name.
   * An English translation of each report's opening and of one representative full text.

   Everything machine-made goes through one reproducible pipeline: `make translate` calls the Anthropic Message Batches API (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`) with a fixed prompt that includes the glossary. It records the model id and prompt hash per item, is resumable, and writes `translations/` (Parquet), which is committed. No computation ever reads it. The site labels these items "machine translation" and always shows the Arabic beside them. A second pass checks a random sample of 200 translations against the Arabic; record the result and fix what it finds.

   Human translations take precedence where their licence allows display. The candidate is HadeethEnc (open with attribution, unmodified text): join where its reports match ours. Do not scrape sunnah.com or reuse the re-hosted copies of publishers' translations found on GitHub; record the licensing reasoning in DECISIONS.

   If this session has no API key, translate the fixture's items yourself so that the pages can be designed against real English, build and test the pipeline, and leave `make translate` for Haseeb to run once.

   **Amended during the session (Haseeb):** there will be no API key. Make the `translations/` file format the contract, with a `producer` field per item that can be a session as well as an API run. Translate the fixture's items yourself as planned. A separate session will fill the full corpus into the same format after this PR merges. Keep `make translate` as an optional API path, but nothing should depend on it.
6. Look and feel. A serious reference work, not a dashboard: calm, typographic, document-like — the clarity of the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy or Our World in Data. Hierarchy comes from type and spacing, not from boxes around everything. Light and dark follow the system. One accent colour. Arabic set in a proper Naskh face at a comfortable size. Tabular numerals. Contrast at WCAG AA, full keyboard use, visible focus.
7. Usability checks — the definition of done. In a real browser, at 1280 px and 390 px wide, perform these as a first-time reader, put the screenshots in the PR, and fix anything that takes more than a minute or needs a term the page does not explain:
   * Type "actions are judged by intentions" → land on its report → state how many independent lines it has with everyone and with trustworthy narrators only, who the bottleneck is, and why its classical grade is ṣaḥīḥ.
   * Find "whoever lies about me deliberately" → state whether it is mutawātir by count and how many companions narrate it.
   * Open al-Zuhrī → state who he was, when he died, his rating, and in how many reports he is the common link.
   * Open a report whose best chain is ḍaʿīf by the classical rules → name the narrator responsible and whether a second chain raises it.
   * Search «من كذب علي متعمدا» in Arabic and get that same report first.
   * Do the first task again on the phone width.
8. Keep: static generation, GitHub Pages, the 1 GB budget and its measurement, the CI site checks (extend them to the new page parts), and `src/itibar/site/`'s data layer. Extend the pipeline where the page needs data it lacks — disjoint lines per threshold, the summary-tree layout, teachers and students, translations, the glossary — with tests.

## Decisions already made

* Claude owns this redesign; the generator's data layer stays.
* English-first interface; the Arabic is always present beside any English.
* Story view by default; the full graph is the specialist view. κ is shown as "independent lines".
* No verdict of the site's own; grades are classical terms with attribution.
* Machine translations are labelled, display-only, reproducible, and never feed a computation.
* Still static on GitHub Pages: no server, no accounts, no analytics.

## Out of scope

New research computation (beyond what a page needs to display existing results); the paper; a custom domain; comments or accounts.
